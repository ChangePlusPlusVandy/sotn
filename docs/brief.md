# **SOTN x Change++ Train4Life Recruitment & Intake Platform**

## **Updated Initial Project Scope Draft**

### **Project Overview**

The current direction of the Train4Life x Change++ project has evolved toward building a centralized recruitment, intake, and participant routing platform for Special Olympics Tennessee (SOTN) and potentially Special Olympics as a whole through other programs. The platform is intended to serve as an organized entry point into the broader T4L and Special Olympics ecosystem for athletes, guardians/caregivers, volunteers, coaches, schools, donors, facilities, and community organizations, among other user groups as well.

Rather than an operational management platform, the system is now expected to help streamline how users:

* Discover programs  
* Enter the organization  
* Determine program fit  
* Access resources  
* Connect to the appropriate systems and contacts

The broader goal is creating a scalable outreach and onboarding pipeline that reduces manual coordination and standardizes participant intake across programs and locations.

The platform may eventually interface with COE infrastructure, SEO NOW operational systems, external onboarding/registration systems, and other SO/SOTN platforms through APIs and shared data pipelines.

---

### **Core Product Direction**

The current product direction centers around a two sided platform structure consisting of a public-facing intake experience and an administrative dashboard.

#### **User Facing Platform**

The user facing side of the platform is intended to function as a public facing intake/onboarding experience. Users would be able to:

* Learn about SOTN/T4L opportunities  
* Submit information about themselves or others  
* Determine eligibility/program fit  
* Access educational resources and onboarding materials  
* Receive next-step guidance  
* Connect with the appropriate programs or contacts

#### **Administrative Dashboard**

The administrative side of the platform is intended to support SOTN staff with intake management, outreach coordination, onboarding oversight, and participant routing workflows.

The dashboard is currently expected to support:

* Participant routing  
* Outreach coordination  
* Contact management  
* Intake organization  
* Referral tracking  
* Notifications/reminders  
* Program onboarding oversight

The broader goal is reducing recruitment and onboarding overhead through centralized workflows and automation.

---

### **Organizational Goals**

Several broader organizational goals were repeatedly emphasized throughout discussions.

#### **Expand Community Reach**

One of the primary motivations behind the platform is increasing awareness and participation throughout Tennessee. Current discussions suggested that many communities remain largely disconnected from Special Olympics Tennessee programming.

The platform is intended to help create more scalable outreach pipelines for schools, recreation centers, therapists/clinics, community organizations, volunteers, coaches, potential athletes, and donors.

#### **Standardize Intake Workflows**

Another major goal is creating a more organized and standardized intake process across programs and locations. Current workflows appear fragmented and heavily manual.

The platform is expected to help standardize:

* Registration  
* Referral routing  
* Program qualification  
* Educational onboarding  
* Follow-up communication

#### **Reduce Administrative Overhead**

Current onboarding and outreach processes require substantial manual coordination. The platform aims to automate repetitive workflows involving notifications/reminders, outreach tracking, educational material distribution, intake tracking, contact summaries, and referral management.

---

### **User Groups**

The platform is currently expected to support a broad range of users, referral sources, and organizations.

#### **Potential Athletes**

Potential athletes, with or without IDD, may enter the platform directly or through referrals from parents/guardians, teachers, schools, therapists, and community organizations.

#### **Guardians/Caregivers**

Guardians and caregivers may enter through the platform directly or through referrals, and assist with onboarding, intake workflows, communication, and connecting athletes to programs and resources.

#### **Volunteers and Coaches**

Volunteers and coaches may use the platform to:

* Learn about opportunities  
* Access onboarding materials  
* Receive educational/training resources  
* Connect with locations and programs

### **Donors**

Potential donors may use the platform to learn about Special Olympics Tennessee initiatives, receive information about relevant programs, and connect with appropriate staff or opportunities for involvement. Similar to other user groups, donors would be routed toward resources, contacts, and next steps based on their interests and level of engagement.

#### **Venues/Locations**

Potential partner organizations and venues may include:

* Unified Champion Schools  
* Recreation centers  
* Facilities/venues  
* Community organizations  
* Referral partners

#### **Administrative Users**

Administrative users would primarily include SO/SOTN administrators, program coordinators, and area managers/directors. These users would oversee intake, outreach, onboarding, referral management, and broader organizational workflows.

---

### **Platform Flow**

The current conceptual user flow is roughly:

User enters platform  
→ submits information  
→ platform evaluates interests, qualifications, involvement type, and program fit  
→ users are placed into user groups, receive educational materials and next step guidance  
→ information is routed to appropriate contacts/programs/systems

Administrative users manage follow-up and onboarding workflows, along with having their own dashboard.

The platform may eventually route information into SEO NOW systems/platforms, COE infrastructure, T4L operational systems, and external onboarding or registration systems.

### **Accessibility and UX Direction**

Accessibility remains a major priority throughout the project.

Current design discussions emphasize strong visual hierarchy, simplified onboarding flows, clear navigation, bold visual design, reduced onboarding confusion, and high levels of information reinforcement.

The current direction is intentionally moving toward a distinct, platform specific visual identity rather than directly replicating existing Special Olympics branding, that being simply red and white.
